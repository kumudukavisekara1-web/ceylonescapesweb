Before You Travel

Key information regarding visas, insurance, financial planning, and weather patterns.

Official Visa & Entry Requirements

All international visitors travelling to Sri Lanka for tourism purposes require a valid passport with a minimum of 6 months validity from the arrival date, along with an official Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) or eVisa prior to departure.

Authoritative Government Portals:

Always verify current entry regulations and obtain official visas directly via:

  • Department of Immigration & Emigration (Official ETA Portal: eta.gov.lk)
  • Department of Immigration & Emigration Sri Lanka
  • Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA)
Beware of unofficial third-party visa processing websites charging excessive surcharges.

Currency, Money & Insurance

The national currency is the Sri Lankan Rupee (LKR). Foreign currency can be legally exchanged at authorized banking counters at Bandaranaike International Airport (CMB) or licensed banks in major towns.

  • Cards: Visa and Mastercard are accepted at all star-class hotels, large supermarkets, and established restaurants. American Express is accepted at select high-end resorts.
  • ATMs: Commercial Bank, Sampath Bank, and Hatton National Bank ATMs are widespread and support international cards with chip-and-PIN.
  • Cash: Keep 100, 500, and 1,000 LKR notes for tuk-tuks, roadside king coconuts, and tip gratuities.
  • Travel Insurance: Comprehensive travel insurance covering medical evacuation, trip cancellation, and safari activities is strongly advised.

Weather & Two-Monsoon Climate

Sri Lanka enjoys tropical warmth year-round (average 27°C–32°C in coastal areas, 14°C–20°C in the central highlands). The island is characterized by two distinct monsoon cycles:

  • South-West Monsoon (Yala): May to September brings rain to the south and west coasts and central hills, while the north and east coasts (Trincomalee, Pasikuda, Jaffna) bask in sunny, dry weather.
  • North-East Monsoon (Maha): October to January brings showers to the north and east, while the south and west coasts (Galle, Bentota, Colombo) enjoy calm seas and clear skies.

When is the Best Time to Visit?

Because of the alternating monsoons, Sri Lanka is a genuine 365-day destination:

  • December – April: Peak season for Colombo, Galle, Bentota, Mirissa, Yala safari, and the hill country.
  • May – September: Ideal season for Trincomalee beach resorts, Pigeon Island diving, Jaffna cultural tours, and the Minneriya Elephant Gathering.
  • July – August: Peak cultural festival period highlighted by the grand Esala Perahera in Kandy.
Island Transit

Getting Around Sri Lanka

Navigating the island safely, comfortably, and scenically.

🚗

Private Chauffeur-Guides

The most flexible and stress-free way for international travellers. Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA) licensed chauffeur-guides provide air-conditioned transport, route agility, and deep cultural commentary.

🚂

Scenic Hill Country Railway

The train journey between Kandy, Nanu Oya (Nuwara Eliya), and Ella is globally celebrated as one of the world's most scenic rail routes, winding through clouds, tea terraces, waterfalls, and viaducts.

🛺

Tuk-Tuks (Three-Wheelers)

Ideal for short urban distances, beach hopping, and town markets. In major cities like Colombo and Kandy, insist on a metered three-wheeler or use mobile apps like PickMe for transparent pricing.

📱

Ride-Hailing & Taxis

Colombo and the southern coastal highway are well served by apps like PickMe and Uber, offering fixed pricing, safety tracking, and card payment convenience.

🚌

Expressway Buses

Modern air-conditioned buses operate across the Southern Expressway (E01) connecting Colombo directly with Galle and Matara in under two hours.

✈️

Domestic Floatplanes

Cinnamon Air operates scheduled and chartered amphibious floatplane flights from Colombo (Peliyagoda/Waterfront) to water aerodromes in Kandy, Dickwella, and Castlereagh reservoir.

Cultural Etiquette & Protocol

Travel Tips & Cultural Etiquette

Observing local social norms ensures respectful and enriching interactions.

Temple Dress Etiquette

When entering Buddhist temples or Hindu kovils, both men and women must cover their shoulders and knees. Footwear and headwear must be removed at the entrance. Modest white or light-coloured attire is considered auspicious and respectful.

Religious Photography Rules

Never pose for photographs with your back turned directly toward a Buddha statue; this is considered deeply disrespectful under Sri Lankan cultural law. Do not touch or climb upon ancient religious carvings or ruins.

Local Customs & Greetings

The traditional greeting is "Ayubowan" ("May you live long"), expressed with palms pressed together in front of the chest with a slight bow. Always use your right hand when giving or receiving money or food.

What to Pack

Breathable cotton/linen clothing, temple-appropriate sarong or white trousers, slip-on shoes for temple floors, a warm sweater for Nuwara Eliya evenings, a universal plug adapter (Types D, G, and M are found), and a reusable water bottle.

Health & Sun Safety

Sri Lanka is certified malaria-free by the WHO. However, dengue fever exists, so apply mosquito repellent in the early morning and late afternoon. Stay hydrated with King Coconuts, drink filtered or boiled water, and apply reef-safe sunscreen.

Safety & Tourist Police

Sri Lanka is widely considered a hospitable and safe destination for solo and family travellers. Dedicated Tourist Police stations operate in Colombo, Galle, Kandy, and Mirissa. Emergency Tourist Police Hotline: 1912.
