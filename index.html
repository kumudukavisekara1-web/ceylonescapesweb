Welcome to Sri Lanka

One Island. Endless Experiences.

Sri Lanka is a compact island destination where remarkable diversity can be experienced within a relatively short journey.

Travellers can explore ancient heritage sites, sacred temples, tropical beaches, tea-covered mountains, wildlife-rich national parks, vibrant cities and distinctive culinary traditions. Whether you seek the peaceful stillness of an ancient rock monastery or the rush of Indian Ocean surf, Sri Lanka offers an authentic encounter with nature, history, and people.

Travel Responsibly: Wildlife Viewing Ethics

Ceylon Escapes actively promotes responsible, non-intrusive wildlife encounters that prioritize animal welfare and habitat preservation over tourist proximity.

Maintain Distance:

Safari jeeps must maintain a minimum distance of 30 metres from elephants and leopards.

Never Feed Wildlife:

Feeding alters natural foraging behaviour and increases human-wildlife conflict.

Respect Quietness:

Keep engines off at sightings and speak in hushed tones to avoid causing wildlife distress.

Licensed Guides:

We partner exclusively with DWC-certified safari naturalists and licensed whale skippers.

Responsible Travel

Travel Responsibly. Leave a Positive Footprint.

Sustainable tourism is a core responsibility. Every journey should enrich local host communities and safeguard delicate ecosystems.

Support Local Communities

Direct your tourism spend toward family-run guesthouses, certified local guides, and community-based dining.

Support Local Businesses

Purchase authentic traditional handicrafts, handloom textiles, and spices directly from island artisans rather than mass factory imports.

Protect Fragile Wildlife

Decline elephant back rides, avoid unlicensed animal selfies, and patronize reputable ethical sanctuaries like Udawalawe Transit Home.

Eliminate Single-Use Plastic

Carry refillable water flasks; Sri Lanka provides abundant filtered refill points across reputable boutique properties.

Respect Sacred Traditions

Cover shoulders and knees when visiting Buddhist and Hindu shrines, remove footwear and headwear, and never pose with your back to Buddha statues.

Protect Coral Ecosystems

Use reef-safe mineral sunscreen and never stand on or touch coral formations in Pigeon Island, Hikkaduwa, or Bar Reef.

Choose Sustainable Stays

Prioritize hotels utilizing solar power, rainwater harvesting, composting, and fair local employment policies.

Follow Park Regulations

Stay strictly on designated national park vehicle tracks to preserve sensitive topsoil and nesting grounds.

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear, practical guidance to help plan your journey to Sri Lanka.

Sri Lanka is truly a year-round destination because its two distinct monsoon systems alternate across the island. The south and west coasts, along with the central hill country, experience their best weather from December to April. Conversely, the east and northern coasts (Trincomalee, Pasikuda, Jaffna) enjoy warm, dry sunshine from May to September. When one coast has rain, the other is in peak season.

To experience the classic highlights (Sigiriya, Kandy, a highland tea estate in Nuwara Eliya or Ella, a wildlife safari, and southern beaches), we recommend a minimum of 7 to 10 days. If you wish to incorporate the cultural north (Jaffna) or deep east coast relaxation (Trincomalee), a 14 to 21-day journey allows for a measured, immersive pace without rushing.

Sri Lanka is widely celebrated as one of the world's premier romantic destinations. It combines luxury colonial tea estate bungalows with private fireplaces, secluded coastal villas with private plunge pools, world-class Ayurvedic couple rituals, and spectacular Indian Ocean sunset dining—all within manageable, scenic transit distances.

Sri Lanka's top wildlife parks include Yala National Park (highest leopard density), Wilpattu National Park (leopards and sloth bears among natural villus), Minneriya & Kaudulla (the Great Elephant Gathering), and Udawalawe National Park (year-round elephant herds). For marine life, Mirissa and Trincomalee offer world-class Blue Whale and dolphin watching.

The foremost cultural landmarks belong to the UNESCO "Cultural Triangle", including the 5th-century Sigiriya Rock Fortress, the sacred cave sanctuaries of Dambulla, the ancient royal ruins of Anuradhapura and Polonnaruwa, the Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic in Kandy, and the Dutch-colonial ramparts of Galle Fort.

The most comfortable, flexible way for international visitors is hiring a private vehicle with an English-speaking licensed chauffeur-guide. Additionally, taking the scenic hill country train between Kandy, Nanu Oya (Nuwara Eliya), and Ella is a bucket-list experience celebrated globally for its panoramic tea plantation vistas.

Pack lightweight, breathable cotton or linen clothing for coastal areas. Bring modest white or light-colored attire that covers shoulders and knees for temple visits. For the hill country (Nuwara Eliya, Ella), temperatures drop in the evening, so pack a light fleece or warm sweater. Sturdy walking shoes, reef-safe sunscreen, and insect repellent are also essential.

The official currency is the Sri Lankan Rupee (LKR). Major credit cards (Visa, Mastercard) are widely accepted in star-class hotels, supermarkets, and established restaurants in tourist towns. It is advisable to carry modest amounts of cash in LKR for local fruit stalls, tuk-tuks, and village donations.
