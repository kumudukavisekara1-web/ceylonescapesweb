Culinary Roots

Traditional Cooking & Spice Alchemy

True Sri Lankan cooking relies on time-honoured implements and indigenous spices cultivated in home gardens across the island.

Curries are cooked in porous earthenware clay pots (walang) over firewood hearths (lipa), infusing the dishes with a distinct earthy smoke. Whole spices—including true Ceylon cinnamon (Cinnamomum verum), green cardamom, cloves, and black pepper—are dry-roasted to a deep mahogany and hand-ground on granite grinding stones (miris gala).

Fresh coconut milk (pol kiri) is hand-extracted in two pressings: the thick first milk (miti kiri) added at the end for rich silkiness, and the lighter second milk (diya kiri) used to gently simmer the vegetables and meat.

Liquid Gold

The Heritage of Pure Ceylon Tea

Introduced to the central highlands in 1867 by Scotsman James Taylor, Ceylon tea grew to become the global benchmark for orthodox tea craftsmanship.

From the mist-draped peaks of Nuwara Eliya to the rolling valleys of Dimbula and Uva, terroir dictates character. The unique topography and alternating monsoon winds allow Sri Lanka to produce distinct regional flavour profiles throughout the calendar year.

At Ceylon Escapes, our guests visit working heritage factories to trace the orthodox journey: from hand-plucking to withering, rolling, fermenting (oxidation), drying, and mechanical sifting into grades such as Orange Pekoe (OP) and Broken Orange Pekoe (BOP).

Ritual & Living Tradition

Festivals, Sacred Dance & Artisans

Explore the ceremonial vibrancy that defines Sri Lankan community life across millennia.

Annual Pageant

Kandy Esala Perahera

One of Asia's oldest and most magnificent cultural processions. Held annually in July/August, featuring hundreds of ceremonial drummers, whip crackers, fire dancers, and richly caparisoned tuskers carrying the sacred relic casket.

Folk Artistry

Mask Carving (Ambalangoda)

Hand-carved from the lightweight timber of the Kaduru tree (Strychnos nux-vomica), traditional masks are categorized into Raksha (demon) masks, Kolam (narrative drama) masks, and Sanni (healing exorcism) masks painted with natural plant dyes.

Coastal Heritage

Traditional Stilt Fishermen

An artisanal fishing method unique to the southern coast around Weligama, Ahangama, and Koggala. Fishermen balance patiently upon a slender crossbar (petta) tied to a vertical wooden pole embedded into shallow coral reefs.

Multi-Faith Island

Spiritual Heritage & Harmonious Living

Sri Lanka has nurtured a pluralistic society where four great world religions coexist in sacred geography.

Buddhist & Hindu Synthesis

Throughout Sri Lanka's historical devalayas and kovils, Buddhist and Hindu spiritual practices intertwine. At Kataragama in the deep south, Buddhists, Hindus, Muslims, and indigenous Vedda communities pay homage at the same sacred forest shrine, embodying the island's deep-rooted tradition of mutual spiritual respect.

Colonial Christian & Islamic Heritage

Colonial-era Catholic churches in Negombo, Dutch Reformed churches inside Galle Fort, and Moorish architectural mosques in Colombo and Galle reflect vibrant trade and intercultural dialogue spanning over a thousand years along maritime Silk Road networks.
